Why the published gender pay gap flatters

Client: Any HR or reward team about to benchmark its first pay gap report against the national figure Eurostat publishes.

Is the national gap the right benchmark, and where will pay transparency bite hardest?

What I did

  • Decomposed the published 2022 gap of each of the 27 Member States into a between-sector and a within-sector part, reconciled with Eurostat's own figures.
  • Set each country's within-sector gap against its exposure to annual reporting: the share of employees in enterprises of 250 or more.
  • Published an article, a working paper and an explorer for every country, all rebuilt from data snapshots in R and Quarto.

What it found

  • Women are over-represented in sectors that pay above average, such as education and health, which lowers the national figure: the lowest headlines hide the most.
  • In all six countries with complete data by enterprise size, the gap is wider in the enterprises of 250 or more, the first to report.

With real data

  • A client's own report set against its sector's within-sector gap, not the national one.
  • The 2026 Structure of Earnings Survey when Eurostat releases it.

How it is checked. Decomposition identities and the reconciliation with Eurostat's published gaps tested in R (64 tests); every table rebuilt from pinned snapshots.
