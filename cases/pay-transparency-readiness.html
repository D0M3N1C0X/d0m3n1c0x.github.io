Is a four-country employer ready for the EU Pay Transparency Directive?

Client: A synthetic employer with 3,443 workers in Italy, Poland, Germany and Spain, about to file its first pay gap reports under Directive (EU) 2023/970.

When do we report, what will the numbers say, where are the gaps and why, and what would closing them cost?

17.6%hourly gender pay gap across the group
12 of 28categories of workers to justify or remedy by 7 Dec 2027
≤ €1.9Ma year to close every flagged gap
1 of 14obligations already in place

What I did

  • Computed the seven Article 9 reporting items and the Article 10 screen for every employer and category of workers, on hourly and on annual pay.
  • Built the categories of equal value with a gender-neutral job evaluation, and tested its weights against the EU guidelines published by the Commission and EIGE in March 2026.
  • Read the Italian decree in the Gazzetta Ufficiale and reported Milan on both measures: the Directive's total pay and Italy's fixed pay (livello retributivo).
  • Delivered a live Excel model, a board briefing, a readiness checklist and a browser tool that runs the same assessment on a client's payroll without the file leaving the computer.

What it found

  • Bonus, commission and allowances add 2.2 points to a 15.4% base-pay gap. In Sales, commission is 12.6% of base pay for women and 15.1% for men under identical rules: territories are allocated unequally.
  • In Italy the decree counts fixed pay only: Milan's gap is 18.0% on that measure against 19.7% on total pay, and what each flagged category has to justify changes.

With real data

  • Calendar-year payroll instead of a snapshot, with leavers and pay after leave.
  • In Italy, the collective agreement's levels as the starting categories (D.lgs. 96/2026, art. 4).
  • A justification workshop per flagged category, before the cost is budgeted.

How it is checked. 511 figures reconciled between the Excel formulas and pandas; the browser tool checked on 3,821 values; every legal statement in a verification register with its source.
